What We Do

Six Things, Done Properly

You asked what services we offer and where our expertise sits. This is the whole list — not a menu we’d learn on your budget.

Operations & process consulting

Mapping how the business actually runs, branch by branch and desk by desk, then redesigning the parts that leak time or margin. Written SOPs, clear handoffs, and a roadmap ordered by what pays back first.

ClickUp business operating systems

One place where the whole business runs — orders, tasks, branch checklists, procurement, franchise onboarding — with automated status flows and live dashboards instead of a WhatsApp group and somebody’s spreadsheet.

Workflow automation

Connecting the systems that don’t talk to each other — Shopify, accounting, stock, email, WhatsApp — using n8n, Make or Zapier, so orders and figures stop being copied by hand from one screen to another.

CRM & sales automation

Every enquiry captured in one pipeline whether it arrived by phone, WhatsApp, Facebook or the website, with automatic follow-up that stops the moment a person replies — so nothing goes cold because someone was busy on the shop floor.

AI agents & chatbots

WhatsApp and website assistants that answer product questions, check availability, take repeat orders and book callbacks around the clock — plus voice agents that handle the calls nobody gets to at month end.

Portals & custom builds

Self-service portals for trade and franchise customers to place their own orders and track them, plus bespoke tools where nothing off the shelf fits — quoting engines, ordering apps, stock request systems.

Most engagements use two or three of these, never all six — which of them applies is exactly what the first conversation is for.

Five ways of selling that each tend to grow their own way of working

Seven branches, one stock question

When a customer in Alberton asks for something that’s out on the shelf, how long does it take to find out whether Diepkloof or Orange Farm has it? In most multi-branch retailers that answer costs a phone call, a WhatsApp and a wait — and the sale often doesn’t survive the delay.

An external sales desk without a trade portal

Your enquiry came from External Sales, but there’s no trade or bulk ordering section on the site. That usually means wholesale orders arrive by phone, WhatsApp and email, get written down, then get typed into a system again — twice the work and one more place for a quantity to go wrong.

The same customer, arriving three ways

Someone who buys in Lenasia on Saturday, orders online on Tuesday and phones the sales desk next month is usually three separate records. Which makes it very hard to know who your best customers actually are, or to treat them like it.

Franchising only scales if it’s repeatable

You’re taking franchise applications, which means the way a Rani’s store runs has to become a documented, teachable system rather than something learned by standing next to someone. That’s process work before it’s software work, and it’s the single highest-leverage thing on this page.

Herbal products get finished

Mixtures, teas, oils and salts run out on a fairly predictable cycle. If nothing reminds that customer at the right moment, the repeat purchase depends entirely on them remembering you — which is the most expensive way to earn revenue you’ve already paid to acquire once.

How long does the month take to close?

Seven branches plus online plus trade usually means a consolidated view of sales arrives days late, assembled by hand. Decisions then get made on last month’s numbers — or on instinct, which is fine until you’re running twelve stores instead of seven.

All of that is guesswork, and we want to be straight about it. Everything above came from your website and a search — not from inside your business. They’re the patterns we see repeatedly in multi-branch retailers who also sell online and to trade, not findings about Rani’s. Some will be wrong. You may have solved three of them already, and you almost certainly have two problems we haven’t thought of, because we haven’t stood behind the counter yet. That’s the entire reason the first piece of real work is an audit rather than a proposal: we’d rather replace these guesses with facts than sell you a solution to a problem you don’t have.
Recommendations

Where We’d Start

If you asked us today, before any audit, this is the order we’d tackle it in — cheapest and fastest payback first, foundations underneath, and the clever stuff last. The audit will almost certainly reshuffle this. We’d rather show you our thinking now than be vague about it.

1

Repeat-purchase reminders

Quickest payback

Mixtures, teas, oils and salts get used up on a fairly predictable cycle, which makes them close to ideal for automated reminders. A message at roughly the right moment — WhatsApp or email, in the customer’s language and easy to opt out of — turns a one-off buyer into a repeat one without anyone lifting the phone.

We put this first because it is the smallest amount of work on this page and the fastest to show a number. It touches nothing else, so it can run while everything below is still being decided, and if it doesn’t perform you’ve lost very little finding out.

2

A trade ordering portal for External Sales

Biggest time saving

Your enquiry came from External Sales, and there’s no trade or bulk ordering section on the website — which usually means wholesale orders arrive by phone, WhatsApp and email, get written down, then get typed into a system a second time. That is slow, it’s where quantity errors come from, and it caps how many accounts one person can carry.

A portal lets trade customers place and track their own orders against their own pricing, and the order lands ready to fulfil rather than needing re-keying. The first thing we’d check is whether Shopify’s own B2B features cover enough of it, because using what you already pay for beats building something custom.

3

ClickUp as the operating system for branches and franchising

Foundation

You’re taking franchise applications, which means how a Rani’s store runs has to become something you can hand to a stranger rather than something learned by standing next to someone. That is the highest-leverage work available to you, and it is process work before it is software.

ClickUp is where we’d put it — franchise onboarding as a repeatable template that fires the same tasks in the same order every time, branch checklists, procurement, and one place where head office can see all seven stores without ringing round. More detail on that below, including what it wouldn’t do.

4

One customer record across all three channels

Foundation

Someone who buys in Lenasia on Saturday, orders online on Tuesday and phones the sales desk next month is currently three separate people as far as your systems are concerned. Joining those up is what tells you who your best customers actually are — and it’s the thing everything further down this list quietly depends on.

5

One dashboard instead of a month-end assembly job

Visibility

Seven branches plus online plus trade usually means the consolidated picture arrives days late and is built by hand. Pulling those sources into one live view means decisions get made on this week’s numbers rather than last month’s — which matters more with every store you add.

6

A WhatsApp assistant — last, deliberately

Later, on purpose

An AI assistant that answers product questions, checks availability and takes repeat orders around the clock is genuinely useful for a business like yours. But it can only ever be as accurate as the data sitting behind it, and an assistant that confidently tells a customer something untrue is worse than no assistant at all.

So this comes after the records are joined up and the stock picture is trustworthy — not because it’s hard to build, but because building it first is how these projects get a bad name. Plenty of agencies would sell it to you today. We’d rather sell it to you when it will work.

Read this as a hypothesis, not a plan. Every item above is inferred from your website and a search — we haven’t seen your stock system, your margins, or how your team actually works. The Fit

Why We’d Point You at ClickUp

Recurring work, across multiple locations, that head office needs to see without asking. That is the exact shape of problem ClickUp is good at — and the exact shape of a seven-branch retailer with a franchise programme.

What it would hold

  • Franchise onboarding as a template — every new store fires the same tasks, in the same order, with the same deadlines, so opening the twelfth is as controlled as opening the eighth
  • Branch routines — opening and closing checklists, stock counts, promotions and merchandising, all visible from head office without a phone call
  • Procurement and supplier orders — a pipeline with stages and owners rather than a chain of messages
  • Product launches — one plan pushed across all seven stores, with proof each one actually did it
  • Staff onboarding and training — particularly useful when a franchisee needs to train people you’ve never met
  • Dashboards — what’s late, what’s blocked, which branch is behind, in a live view rather than a report someone assembles

What it would not replace

  • Your Shopify store — ClickUp is not an e-commerce platform and shouldn’t pretend to be. Shopify stays where it is; we connect them
  • Your point-of-sale — tills keep doing what tills do
  • Your accounting package — ClickUp is not a general ledger and using it as one ends badly
  • Your stock system of record — ClickUp can trigger and track the work around stock, but it should not be the place inventory numbers actually live
  • Customer conversations — WhatsApp with customers stays customer-facing; ClickUp is the internal side
Two things we should declare. First, we’re a ClickUp partner — it’s the platform we implement most, and we can supply licences directly at partner pricing, which is usually better than buying alone. That also means we have something to gain when we recommend it, and you should weigh what we say accordingly. Second, and precisely because of that: if the audit says your real problem is a stock system, or a better till integration, or simply three people doing a job that should be one person’s, we’ll tell you that instead. A ClickUp implementation on top of an unsolved process problem is an expensive way to be disappointed, and we’d rather lose the licence revenue than sell you one.
